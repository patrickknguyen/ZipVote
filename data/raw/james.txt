# Andrea James — Issue Positions & Biography


## Campaign site: https://www.ajforma.com/housing-affordability

#### HOUSING AFFORDABILITY
Massachusetts is in a housing crisis. Rents have surged, homeownership is increasingly out of reach, and homelessness is rising. Housing has been treated primarily as an investment asset rather than a human necessity.
As Governor, I will declare a statewide housing emergency, create a Social Housing Authority to build permanently affordable housing, use state-owned land to build tens of thousands of homes, support rent stabilization policies, tie state housing funding to tenant protections, and tax luxury real estate speculation and large corporate landlords.
#### Housing Funding Strategy
Housing construction will be financed through public capital bonding, progressive real estate taxation on luxury speculation and corporate landlords, and the use of state-owned land to reduce development costs.
#### Housing FAQs
#### How will you pay to build tens of thousands of homes?
Housing construction will be financed through infrastructure bonding, progressive real estate taxes, and state-owned land use. The state already spends large sums managing housing crises; this plan adds a permanent housing supply.
#### Will your plan cut programs like RAFT or housing vouchers?
No. These programs prevent homelessness and will be protected.
#### Won’t rent stabilization discourage housing construction?
Rent stabilization protects tenants while large-scale housing construction increases supply.

## Campaign site: https://www.ajforma.com/universal-healthcare

#### UNIVERSAL HEALTHCARE: An Act Establishing Medicare for All in Massachusetts
Establishes a single-payer health care system in Massachusetts
Provides health care for all Massachusetts residents and for all non-residents who work 20 hours or more per week in the state, without premiums, co-pays, or deductibles
Bans private insurance coverage for services already covered by the single-payer system
Healthcare should be a public good. Massachusetts residents still face high premiums, deductibles, and prescription costs.
I will pursue a state-level universal healthcare system, seek federal waivers to pool Medicaid and ACA funding, cap hospital and pharmaceutical costs, and expand coverage using existing state healthcare infrastructure.
#### Healthcare Funding Strategy
Funding combines existing public healthcare spending, federal participation, stronger cost controls, and reduced insurance administrative overhead.
#### Would universal healthcare require major tax increases?
No. Residents already pay enormous healthcare costs through premiums and deductibles. Reform reorganizes spending so more money goes to care. Under universal healthcare, individuals and businesses would actually see a significant saving.
For more information on Single Payer Healthcare, please follow the work of masscare.org at https://masscare.org/about/

## Campaign site: https://www.ajforma.com/universal-childcare

#### Free Childcare for All
Childcare is an essential economic infrastructure and increases workforce participation and economic productivity.
Healey’s $31.2M is being framed as “affordable childcare,” but covers only about 2,000 children on the income-eligible waitlist. That means it’s not universal
Childcare costs in Massachusetts are among the highest in the nation, with center-based infant care often exceeding $20,000 annually. I’ve been a single mom, and I know firsthand the challenges families face and how childcare not only helped me make ends meet when I was a young mother but also helped me get a college education and expand opportunities for my family.
Today, families are still struggling to afford childcare, and more than 31,000 families have applied for assistance and are waitlisted for years. The current administration recently announced adding an additional $31.2 million to make childcare more affordable and accessible; however, that is a drop in the bucket, albeit it will come as an immense relief for one of the approximately 2,000 families who may be deemed eligible. That still leaves approximately 28,000 families in need, and too many out of the workforce and out of educational opportunities due to unaffordable childcare. And that’s just the families who have applied.
So yes, $31.2M helps some families, but it does not come close to solving the crisis. Massachusetts has around 350,000 children under 5, and childcare averages more than $26,000 a year.
As Governor, I will expand publicly funded childcare centers, grow the childcare workforce, and raise wages for childcare workers. We should provide dedicated state-level tax credits to employers that offer childcare benefits.
#### Funding Strategy
Childcare investment increases workforce participation and economic growth. Every family has different childcare needs, and they should be able to choose what is right for them. As one of the wealthiest states in the country, Massachusetts must grow its
childcare workforce and should provide quality childcare for free to our families. The more parents who join or rejoin the workforce, the more that goes back into our economy.

## Campaign site: https://www.ajforma.com/education

#### EDUCATION
For more than a year, I have traveled across Massachusetts, visiting schools and talking with parents, educators, and students. As the parent of a child in a public school that is already underfunded, facing teacher layoffs, and losing educational programs, I have seen these challenges firsthand. I've also visited schools with abundant resources, offering Advanced Placement courses, arts programs, athletics, electives, and other opportunities that many communities can only dream of. The disparities are impossible to ignore. The Healey administration often points to Massachusetts' top ranking on the National Assessment of Educational Progress (NAEP) as evidence that our education system is the nation's best. But statewide averages don't tell the whole story. They can mask the significant differences in resources, opportunities, and outcomes across school districts in the Commonwealth.
However, this average masks extreme, well-documented disparities across the state. The dual narrative exists because:
Averages vs. Demographics: While affluent suburban districts (like Needham and Wellesley) continue to soar past pre-pandemic scores, poorer, more diverse urban districts (like Lynn, Everett, and Revere) show lagging or dropping scores.
Statewide vs. Subgroup: Massachusetts often ranks first when evaluating overall student averages, but significant achievement and reading gaps persist among students of color, low-income students, and English language learners. For instance, a report revealed that over 60% of Massachusetts schools remain segregated, which heavily correlates with funding and performance disparities.
#### The problem with MCAS wasn’t its name
Massachusetts voters made their voices heard in 2024, with nearly 60% voting to end MCAS as a high school graduation requirement. Many saw it as an opportunity to move away from high-stakes testing and toward graduation requirements that better reflect student learning. Now, new statewide graduation proposals include standardized end-of-course exams, a move the Massachusetts Teachers Association argues goes against what voters approved. According to the MTA, Question 2 ended 30 years of high-stakes testing and created an opportunity to rethink graduation requirements, but this administration's new proposal risks replacing one statewide testing requirement with another.
Whether you support standardized testing or not, the larger question is one of accountability: when voters make a decision at the ballot box, should state leaders advance policies that closely resemble what voters chose to end?
Education should open doors for every student, not limit their future. Our public schools deserve meaningful investment, and every graduate should leave high school with a clear pathway to success, whether that's college, a career, an apprenticeship, or another opportunity. Right now, too many students are graduating without those pathways in place.
As Governor, I will expand K-12 community schools that provide comprehensive educational opportunities for all students with on-campus services focused on helping those families who need it, with the resources to be healthy and thriving as their children receive truly the best education in the country, that’s not just a deceptive slogan. I will invest in trade schools and high school work study opportunities for students who must work during their high school years, and make college education at state schools free.
#### Education Funding Strategy
Funding will combine Fair Share revenue, restored state higher-ed funding, and improved financial aid.
#### Education FAQs
Fair Share helps, but is not the only funding source. The state must restore public investment in higher education.

#### ABOLISH ICE
Massachusetts should be a place where immigrants feel safe and receive the due process guaranteed under the U.S. Constitution.
As Governor, I will end state cooperation with federal overreach and protect families. I will end all agreements between the Department of Corrections and ICE, ending the transfers of prisoners into ICE custody upon sentence completion. People being released from prison should be guaranteed the same due process protections, and there should not be a blanket agreement for transfers from prison to ICE. People change. Most grow. ## Campaign site: https://www.ajforma.com/labor-rights

#### STANDING WITH WORKERS
Workers deserve fair wages and the freedom to organize. Over the last year, I’ve visited Massachusetts workers on the picket line. Many have shared with me why they made the tough decision to go out on strike. Some described poor working conditions, no sick pay, and having to use vacation days if they needed a day off to get well. They described to me the working conditions with old, damaged equipment. These are workers, our neighbors, the backbone of our Commonwealth, who have worked for the billion-dollar companies for decades and still aren’t paid enough to make ends meet without consistently working overtime.
As Governor, I will raise the minimum wage, ban captive-audience union-busting, require labor peace agreements for state contractors, and support worker organizing. I will drive down the cost of housing, childcare, and utilities, and fight to create public banking to help working families become homeowners.

## Campaign site: https://www.ajforma.com/lgbtq-protections

#### LGBTQ+ PROTECTIONS
Massachusetts must codify protections for gender-affirming healthcare and strengthen anti-discrimination protections. Advancing LGBTQIA+ equality starts with ensuring rights are not just promised but protected in people’s everyday lives.
Massachusetts has a history of leading on these issues, but leadership requires action, especially at a time when rights are being challenged across the country. I will work to expand access to affirming healthcare, invest in mental health supports, and partner with community-based organizations that are already doing the work on the ground. I will also ensure that data, policy, and funding decisions reflect the real needs of LGBTQIA+ people, particularly those who are most impacted, including Black and Brown communities, trans people, and those navigating poverty or housing instability.
This is about building a Commonwealth where dignity, safety, and opportunity are not conditional. A government that truly works for all people protects the right to be who you are, without fear, and backs that up with action.
As governor, I will codify protections for gender-affirming healthcare and strengthen anti-discrimination protections. I will strengthen and enforce nondiscrimination protections in housing, healthcare, employment, and education, and ensure that state agencies are accountable when those protections are violated. That includes safeguarding access to gender-affirming care, protecting LGBTQIA+ youth in our schools, and making sure every person can live safely and openly in their community.

## Campaign site: https://www.ajforma.com/ai-and-data-centers

#### AI AND DATA CENTERS
#### Artificial Intelligence
The choice of our next Governor is critical to whether AI harms or helps the people of Massachusetts.
With AI capabilities advancing exponentially and Massachusetts, specifically, being home to world-class research universities and a growing cluster of AI companies, a measured, regulated approach is required to ensure that smart guardrails are built now. Critics across the political spectrum have taken issue with the federal administration's partnership with OpenAI, largely due to the company's ties to Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) and other federal agencies. This alignment with federal policy has fueled broader concerns here in MA, as many citizens and advocacy groups have expressed distrust over integrating the same corporate AI tools into state government that have already sparked internal resistance among OpenAI employees and ignited
national protests over mass surveillance and military ties.
Providing no transparency, the Healey Administration committed Massachusetts to a three-year, multimillion-dollar contract with OpenAI to deploy its AI tool for the Executive Branch’s 40,000 employees. Government contracts with companies like OpenAI must be transparent about how AI is being deployed and consider input from state employees. Many citizens and advocates express distrust over integrating the same corporate AI tools into state government that have sparked internal OpenAI employee resistance regarding mass surveillance and military ties.
As your Governor, I will keep transparency at the forefront and not dismiss the concerns and distrust of the Commonwealth’s labor force and advocacy groups. I will keep the well-being of the labor of the Commonwealth at the forefront.
#### Data Centers
As home to leading research institutions, Massachusetts has a unique role to play in ensuring that the growth of data centers does not come without accountability. I support a moratorium on building future data centers, listening to the concerns of residents and advocates citing utility bill increases, environmental strain from fossil fuel use, and other environmental and climate threats, and state and federal regulatory lags. As Governor of Massachusetts, I will study the impacts of infrastructure and how to redesign utility rate structures to protect everyday consumers.
I will rescind the current administration’s sales and use tax exemption for data centers. I will work with the legislature to require companies proposing data centers to pay for any increased energy or water consumption costs, so they aren't passed on to consumers. Consumers require that companies pay for any grid improvements incurred and that all projects be subject to strong labor standards.

#### JUSTICE AND FREEDOM
The racial wealth gap in Massachusetts is one of the starkest in the United States. A landmark study by the Federal Reserve Bank of Boston revealed that while white households in Greater Boston had a median net worth of roughly $247,500, U.S.-born Black households had a median net worth close to zero.
This staggering disparity is rooted in systemic economic barriers, and several key factors highlight the localized challenges Black residents face in Massachusetts today.
As Governor, I will support and work toward building the comprehensive framework for equity, reparative justice, and community investment as developed by the Black Agenda Coalition and outlined in the State of Black Boston 2026 Report, as well as ending the overall racial wealth gap in Massachusetts.

## Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_C._James

Andrea C. James is an American human rights activist. A former attorney, she was disbarred and sentenced to two years' imprisonment in 2009. Since her release, James has called for reform of the criminal justice system, including the end of the incarceration of women.
#### Biography
James was born and raised in Boston, Massachusetts. She graduated with a bachelor's from University of Massachusetts, Boston and a Juris Doctor degree from Northeastern University, returning to Boston to work as a criminal defence and real estate conveyance attorney in Roxbury.
In 2008, James was temporarily suspended as a licensed attorney after being accused of misappropriating client funds. In 2009, she was sentenced to 24 months in federal prison on charges of wire fraud. In 2010, James was formally disbarred by the Supreme Court of Suffolk County.
James served her sentence at the Federal Correctional Institution, Danbury in Danbury, Connecticut. She subsequently stated that conditions in the prison were poor, and that prisoners were treated with "scorn and rebuke" by prison staff. Following her sentence, James was ordered to pay $1,141,861.22, in addition to serving three years of supervised release. James' attempt to appeal the fine and her disbarment on the basis of her previous work supporting disadvantaged communities was unsuccessful.
Following her release, James founded and served as the executive director of Families for Justice as Healing (FJAH), a non-governmental organisation that advocated for criminal justice reform through prioritising community wellness alternatives over incarceration. FJAH had started as a meeting between James and other women while they were imprisoned at FCI Danbury. FJAH also sought to draw attention to women becoming the fastest-growing incarcerated population. FJAH rejects criminalisation and incarceration in response to drug offences and advocates for a shift to "community wellness" to address poverty, addiction and trauma.
As part of her work with FJAH, James met with then- President of the United States, Barack Obama, and Valerie Jarrett, and supported 50 women whose sentences had been commuted by the Obama administration. James subsequently criticised Obama for being "inactive" on addressing the mass incarceration of women, and launched the "100 Women in 100 Days" campaign in response.
On 21 June 2014, FJAH led the FREE HER rally on the National Mall in Washington, D.C. to raise awareness of the impact of overly harsh drug crime sentencing on women and families.
James serves as the founder executive director of the National Council for Incarcerated and Formerly Incarcerated Women. The organisation believes the prison does not support women to heal and advance their lives and causes trauma to them and their children and focuses on transformative justice, alternative community economic development, participatory budgeting and developing liberation projects.
In 2021, James launched the #FreeHer campaign, pushing the administration of Joe Biden to grant clemency to 100 identified incarcerated women whom James felt had been imprisoned for too long.
In 2015, James was announced as a Soros Justice Fellow. In 2016, she won the Robert F. Kennedy Human Rights Award for her activism.
In May 2025, James filed paperwork to run in 2026 as governor of Massachusetts as a Democrat, stating to the Boston Globe she was "exploring" a run.
James is the author of the 2013 Upper Bunkies Unite: And Other Thoughts on the Politics of Mass Incarceration.
